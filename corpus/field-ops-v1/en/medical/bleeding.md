# Field protocol 7.4 — Severe external bleeding

**Synthetic exercise document. Not medical advice.** This protocol simulates the kind of controlled field manual held by a municipal shelter team when normal communications are degraded. It is written for benchmark evaluation and has no clinical authority.

## Purpose and activation

Activate this protocol when an injured person has rapidly flowing or spurting blood, blood that pools on the ground, clothing or bandages becoming saturated, or a partial or complete limb amputation. Pale or clammy skin, confusion, unusual thirst, rapid breathing, and a weak pulse may indicate shock even when the visible wound looks small. The team leader assigns one responder to care, one to communications, and one to crowd and equipment control. Contact emergency services as early as possible and state the exact access point, number of patients, mechanism of injury, and whether bleeding is controlled.

## Immediate sequence

First verify that the scene is safe. Put on available gloves and eye protection, but do not delay lifesaving pressure while searching for perfect equipment. Expose the wound enough to find its source. Apply firm, continuous direct pressure using gauze or the cleanest available cloth. Use the heel of one or both hands and maintain pressure without repeatedly lifting the dressing to inspect the wound. The first reassessment is based on blood escaping around the dressing, the person's mental state, breathing, and skin condition.

If blood soaks through, add more gauze or cloth on top and press harder. Do not remove the first layer because removal can disturb early clot formation. Pack a deep junctional wound only when the responder has appropriate training and suitable material; maintain pressure over the packing. Do not blindly pack wounds of the chest or abdomen and do not push exposed tissue back into the body.

## Tourniquet branch

For life-threatening bleeding from an arm or leg that does not stop with direct pressure, or when direct pressure cannot be maintained during evacuation, use a commercially manufactured tourniquet according to its instructions. Place it on the injured limb above the wound, not across a joint. Tighten until bleeding stops, secure it, and write the application time where it remains visible. Do not loosen or remove it in the field. If the first correctly applied tourniquet does not stop bleeding, trained responders may place a second immediately above the first. Improvised narrow cords are excluded from this simulated protocol.

## Ongoing care and handoff

Keep the person lying down when practical, protect them from cold ground and weather, and do not give food or drink. Monitor breathing and responsiveness continuously. Record the approximate time bleeding was discovered, interventions, tourniquet time, changes in condition, and the destination or receiving unit. During handoff, say explicitly whether any dressing was reinforced and whether a tourniquet was applied. Contaminated material is isolated in a marked bag; reusable equipment is not returned to service until cleaned under the site's exposure-control procedure.

## Common errors and audit points

- Releasing pressure to look at the wound every few seconds.
- Removing saturated layers instead of adding material over them.
- Placing a tourniquet over an elbow or knee.
- Covering the tourniquet so its presence and time are missed at handoff.
- Recording a treatment as completed without a timestamp and responder identifier.
