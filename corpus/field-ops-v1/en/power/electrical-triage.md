# Facilities SOP 4.5 — Building electrical triage after flooding

**Synthetic exercise document. Not electrical guidance.** This invented SOP tests retrieval among generator, refrigeration, and electrical-safety material. Only qualified personnel may assess real electrical systems.

## Safety boundary

Treat every conductor and piece of equipment as energized until an authorized qualified person establishes otherwise. Water on the floor, damaged service equipment, fallen conductors, burning odor, arcing, buzzing, heat, or smoke establishes an exclusion zone. Unqualified shelter staff do not enter to reach a panel, touch a wet disconnect, reset a breaker, or move a fallen cable. Call emergency services for fire, active arcing, or immediate life threat and notify the utility when supply equipment may be involved.

The facilities lead records the observed condition from a safe location, isolates public access, and identifies upstream controls without operating them. Personal protective equipment does not turn an unqualified person into an electrical worker. A dry wooden object, rubber boots, or ordinary gloves are not accepted as proof of protection.

## Assessment by qualified personnel

The qualified assessor receives the site one-line diagram, flood extent, maximum water line, outage history, generator or photovoltaic connections, and known modifications. Before inspection, identify every possible source: utility service, generator, uninterruptible power supply, battery system, solar array, backfeed, and adjacent shared service. Establish an electrically safe work condition using the applicable lockout and verification process.

Document panels, switchgear, receptacles, motors, appliances, controls, life-safety systems, and wiring methods exposed to water or contaminants. Equipment may require replacement even after it looks dry. Energization decisions follow manufacturer, authority, and inspection requirements; drying time alone is not a release criterion. Contaminated floodwater can leave conductive or corrosive residue inside assemblies.

## Load restoration priorities

After authorization, restore circuits in a controlled sequence. Fire alarm, emergency communications, required ventilation, essential medication refrigeration, and designated medical loads receive priority according to the facility continuity plan. Each circuit is identified before operation. Observe current, voltage, protective-device behavior, odor, sound, and heat after energization. An unexplained trip is investigated; it is never defeated or repeatedly reset to “see if it holds.”

Portable generators remain governed by the separate generator deployment SOP. A building circuit is not energized from a portable generator through a receptacle. Transfer equipment must prevent unintended interconnection with the utility. Extension cords are temporary distribution, not a substitute for repairing submerged wiring.

## Occupancy decision

Partial power does not automatically permit reoccupation. The incident lead considers lighting, fire detection, egress, sanitation, ventilation, accessible routes, communications, and the ability to isolate unsafe zones. Clearly mark circuits and rooms that remain out of service. Staff receive a briefing on temporary restrictions and the method for reporting heat, odor, flicker, noise, shock sensation, or repeated trips.

## Records and handoff

The electrical record includes assessor qualifications, source isolation, test instruments, calibration status, inspected equipment, disposition, photographs, replaced components, circuit restoration times, readings, and restrictions. Temporary repairs are labeled with owner and expiration. Shift handoff identifies every energized source and locked-out component. Final closeout requires approved inspection, updated drawings where configuration changed, and removal of temporary distribution.
