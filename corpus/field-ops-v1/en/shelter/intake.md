# Shelter procedure 6.1 — Registration, intake, and privacy

**Synthetic exercise document.** This invented recordkeeping procedure simulates a humanitarian shelter manual. It contains no real people or policy and is intended only to evaluate LastLight.

## Intake objective

Intake establishes accountability and identifies immediate support without turning shelter access into an investigation. A greeter first checks for urgent medical distress, immediate safety threats, family separation, and a need for communication assistance. Life-safety care comes before form completion. Interpreters and accessible formats are offered where available, and the purpose of each requested field is explained.

Create one household record containing household name or chosen reference name, headcount, age bands rather than unnecessary full birth dates, accessibility needs, communication needs, essential medication refrigeration, dietary or allergy information relevant to shelter service, and a reunification contact. Record the minimum useful detail. Do not record immigration status. Do not copy identity documents unless a separately authorized program requires it; lack of identification does not by itself deny emergency shelter in this simulated scenario.

## Identification and accommodation

Give each household a paper identifier linked to the intake record. Explain that it supports meals, reunification, and accountability and is not proof of legal identity. Avoid displaying sensitive needs on a visible badge. Assign sleeping space with attention to mobility, service animals, caregivers, family unity, safeguarding, and proximity to accessible toilets. Medication requiring refrigeration is transferred only to the controlled storage process with a receipt; intake staff do not handle or advise on dosage.

## Orientation

Every household receives a brief orientation covering fire exits, assembly areas, quiet hours, sanitation and handwashing stations, meal times, charging rules, children and vulnerable-person supervision, and how to report a safeguarding concern. Point out accessible routes rather than merely describing them. Ask the household to repeat the fire assembly location and reporting route so misunderstandings can be corrected.

## Privacy and access control

Paper forms remain in a staffed, closed storage point; electronic copies use approved access controls. Volunteers see only the information needed for their role. Public census boards show aggregate counts, never names or medical needs. Requests from media, neighbors, employers, or estranged relatives are referred to the privacy lead. A reunification contact is not automatically authorized to receive all information.

At shift handoff, communicate unresolved operational needs without reading sensitive records over an open radio channel. Correct errors with a dated notation rather than erasing the original entry. Suspected loss or disclosure of records is reported immediately, with the time, material affected, and containment action.

## Updates, departure, and retention

Reconcile headcount at scheduled intervals and after evacuation alarms. When a household leaves, record time, known destination only if voluntarily provided, identifier return, and any open reunification action. Do not announce a person's departure publicly. The shelter manager closes duplicate records and approves final aggregate counts. Records follow the exercise retention schedule and are securely destroyed when that period ends.

## Quality checks

Supervisors sample records for missing headcount, inaccessible accommodation, excessive personal data, visible sensitive notes, unreceipted refrigerated medicine, and absent orientation confirmation. Performance is assessed on safe access and data minimization, not on collecting the largest possible file.
