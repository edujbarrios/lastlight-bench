# Evacuation procedure 8.2 — Route assessment and release

**Synthetic benchmark document. Not operational guidance.** The criteria below are fictional and must not be used to select or open a real evacuation route.

## Purpose and decision authority

Route assessment provides a repeatable picture of whether a proposed corridor can support the assigned movement. A route is not “safe” merely because one vehicle passed through it. The movement lead defines origin, destination, population, vehicle types, accessibility requirements, expected duration, weather window, and alternate corridor. The infrastructure lead designates qualified assessors and is the only role in this exercise authorized to release a route for general movement.

Before deployment, assessors receive current maps, known closures, bridge and culvert records, utility hazards, flood predictions, law-enforcement restrictions, and communications instructions. A map label, old inspection, or crowd report is treated as a lead rather than current proof. Teams record the exact observation time because fire, water, debris, congestion, and structural conditions can change quickly.

## Field assessment sequence

Approach from the known side and stop before entering uncertain terrain. Record surface condition, lane width, shoulder stability, overhead clearance, grade, standing or moving water, debris, smoke, visibility, downed lines, unstable trees, damaged structures, hazardous-material indicators, and public behavior. Do not enter moving floodwater, pass under energized conductors, cross an unassessed bridge, or move a barricade placed by another authority.

For each restriction, document coordinates or a reproducible landmark, photographs when permitted, estimated length, direction affected, vehicle limitation, and whether a turnaround exists. “Passable” must name the tested vehicle class and direction. A corridor that accepts a light assessment vehicle may still reject a bus, ambulance, high-clearance truck, wheelchair transport, or fuel tanker.

## Capacity and accessibility

The movement plan estimates loading rate, intersection control, pedestrian separation, rest points, fuel range, recovery capability, and accessible transport. Assessors identify steep grades, missing curb access, broken surfaces, narrow gates, low branches, and transfer points that could exclude people using mobility devices. Accessibility problems are operational constraints, not optional notes.

Bridge load limits, pavement capacity, and structural stability come from the responsible authority or qualified engineering assessment. Field teams do not calculate a new bridge rating from visual appearance. If the assigned vehicle exceeds documented limits or the limit is unavailable, the route remains restricted until authoritative information is obtained.

## Release, monitoring, and withdrawal

The release record states route version, allowed direction, vehicle classes, speed or spacing controls, opening time, review time, hazards, control points, and alternate. Controllers monitor conditions and report changes using the route identifier. Heavy rain, rising water, new debris, fire movement, loss of traffic control, structural change, collision, or communications failure can trigger suspension.

When a route is withdrawn, controllers stop new entry, account for movements already inside, and distribute the closure through every active channel. An alternate is not activated automatically unless it has its own current release. The log retains assessments, approvals, public messages, closures, and the reason for every status change.
