# Field protocol 7.9 — Respiratory distress recognition and support

**Synthetic benchmark document. Not medical advice.** It must not be used for diagnosis or treatment.

## Recognition and immediate escalation

Respiratory distress may present as rapid or labored breathing, inability to speak full sentences, unusual noisy breathing, chest retractions, bluish or gray color, confusion, exhaustion, or collapse. Call emergency services early for severe symptoms, sudden onset, suspected airway swelling, chest pain, altered mental status, significant smoke or chemical exposure, or deterioration despite the person's usual prescribed rescue plan.

Move only when the environment is unsafe. Smoke, gas, low oxygen, chemical vapor, and electrical hazards require scene control and the appropriate response team. An unprotected responder does not enter a contaminated atmosphere. Report the exposure location, material if known, number of affected people, wind or ventilation conditions, and decontamination status.

## Supportive actions

Help an alert person sit in the position in which breathing is easiest, commonly upright. Loosen restrictive clothing, reduce crowding, and speak calmly. Do not force the person to lie flat. If they have their own prescribed rescue medication and normally self-administer it, assist only within training, local policy, and dispatcher direction. Do not give someone else’s medication or improvise a dose.

Monitor responsiveness, breathing pattern, ability to speak, skin color, and symptom change. A pulse oximeter reading, if available, is contextual information and does not overrule severe clinical signs. Cold fingers, movement, poor circulation, nail products, and device limitations can distort a reading. Staff do not delay escalation while trying repeatedly to obtain a normal number.

## Airway obstruction branch

If a conscious person cannot cough, speak, or breathe because of suspected choking, activate the trained choking response and emergency system. A person who can cough forcefully is encouraged to continue while monitored. Blind finger sweeps are prohibited. If the person becomes unresponsive, lower them safely, begin the trained resuscitation sequence, and use an automated external defibrillator when indicated.

Swelling of lips or tongue, widespread hives with breathing difficulty, or rapid deterioration after a likely allergen suggests a time-critical allergic emergency. Use only the person's prescribed device or authorized stock under the applicable protocol and call emergency services. This synthetic document intentionally does not specify medication dose.

## Oxygen and equipment boundary

Supplemental oxygen is used only by trained and authorized personnel under the applicable protocol. Keep oxygen away from flames, smoking, oil, grease, and damaged regulators. Secure cylinders against falling. A depleted, leaking, contaminated, or out-of-test cylinder is removed from service and labeled.

## Handoff and documentation

Record onset, activity, possible exposure, known history voluntarily provided, prescribed medication used, observed response, monitoring values with times, and changes in speech or alertness. At handoff state whether there was smoke, chemical exposure, choking, allergic signs, chest pain, or loss of consciousness. Preserve privacy by transmitting only information needed for care.
