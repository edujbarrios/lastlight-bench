# Field protocol 7.6 — Thermal and electrical burn response

**Synthetic exercise document. Not medical advice.** This material exists solely to evaluate retrieval and must not guide real patient care.

## Scene control and classification

Stop the burning process without becoming another casualty. For flame or hot-surface injuries, move away from the source and extinguish burning clothing. For an electrical injury, do not touch the person until the energy source has been isolated by an authorized party; high-voltage scenes require a larger controlled area. Chemical exposure follows the substance-specific decontamination plan and is not treated as an ordinary thermal burn.

Call emergency services for burns involving breathing difficulty, the face or airway, major joints, hands, feet, genitals, a large body area, deep tissue, electricity, chemicals, explosion, or vulnerable patients. Smoke exposure in an enclosed space, soot around the mouth, hoarseness, or worsening cough raises concern for airway injury even when skin burns look limited.

## Cooling and protection

For a recent minor thermal burn, cool the area with cool running water for 20 minutes when this can be done without delaying urgent evacuation or causing whole-body chilling. Remove rings, watches, belts, and loose clothing before swelling develops, but do not pull away fabric stuck to skin. Do not use ice, very cold water, butter, oils, toothpaste, powders, or adhesive dressings.

After cooling, cover loosely with a clean non-fluffy dressing or appropriate clean film according to responder training. Keep the rest of the person warm. Do not break blisters and do not apply creams in this simulated field protocol. A wet dressing is not left in place so long that it causes chilling.

## Electrical injury branch

Electrical burns may show small skin marks while causing deeper injury. Once the source is confirmed isolated, assess responsiveness and breathing. Begin resuscitation and use an automated external defibrillator when indicated and available, following prompts. A person exposed to significant current receives urgent professional assessment even if they feel well. Record the suspected source, voltage if reliably known, contact points, loss of consciousness, fall, chest symptoms, and resuscitation.

Do not approach downed power lines or assume a line is dead because it is quiet. Utility lines can re-energize automatically. Keep people and vehicles away and follow utility or emergency-service directions.

## Monitoring and transport

Watch for breathing changes, reduced alertness, pale or clammy skin, increasing pain, swelling, and circulation problems beyond a circumferential injury. Elevate a burned limb only when compatible with other injuries and current guidance. Do not give food or drink when urgent anesthesia or impaired consciousness may be concerns. Avoid estimating burn depth beyond training; describe visible findings and body locations.

## Documentation and infection control

Record time of injury, mechanism, source control, cooling start and duration, removed items, dressings, symptoms, and changes. Preserve information about chemicals or equipment for responders without bringing contaminated objects into a clean area. Gloves and contaminated dressings follow exposure-control procedures. At handoff, state explicitly whether the injury was electrical, chemical, or associated with smoke inhalation.
