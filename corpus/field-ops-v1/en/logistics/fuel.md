# Logistics SOP 5.2 — Emergency fuel receipt, storage, and issue

**Synthetic exercise document. Not hazardous-material guidance.** All roles, quantities, and controls are invented to test offline retrieval.

## Scope and accountability

This SOP covers packaged fuel received for approved generators, vehicles, pumps, and heating equipment. The logistics lead appoints a fuel custodian and an alternate. Every receipt and issue is logged by product, quantity, container identifier, time, supplier or recipient, intended asset, and two-person verification. Fuel is never issued into an unmarked container or for an asset absent from the authorization list.

Before accepting a delivery, compare shipping papers, product labels, seals, container condition, and ordered grade. Quarantine leaking, bulging, badly corroded, contaminated, or mismatched containers without opening them. A discrepancy is photographed and reported. The receiving team does not blend products to correct an error.

## Storage layout

Use the designated outdoor controlled area with ventilation, security, vehicle protection, spill containment, fire separation, and drainage controls established by the site safety plan. Keep fuel away from occupied tents, ignition sources, emergency exits, food, medicine, oxidizers, and storm drains. Containers remain closed, upright, labeled, and protected from damage. Empty containers are treated as containing hazardous vapor.

Post no-smoking and restricted-access signs. Bonding, grounding, fire extinguishers, and electrical classifications follow product and authority requirements; this synthetic procedure does not specify them. Daily inspection covers leaks, odor, water intrusion, damaged labels, tampering, containment capacity, extinguisher status, and inventory variance.

## Issue and transfer

Verify the receiving asset and fuel type before opening storage. Stop engines where the equipment procedure requires it, control ignition sources, and position spill materials. Use approved pumps, nozzles, and containers. Never start a siphon by mouth. An operator remains at the transfer point; no latch or improvised prop is used to leave a nozzle unattended.

Record the pre- and post-transfer quantity or meter reading. Close caps, inspect for drips, return equipment, and update remaining inventory immediately. Generator refueling also follows the generator SOP: stop the engine and allow it to cool before adding fuel. Fuel issue priorities are approved life-safety power, communications, water operations, and essential transport; convenience use is excluded during shortage.

## Spill, fire, and exposure response

Stop transfer if this can be done safely, keep people away, eliminate ignition sources only when safe, protect drains with available barriers, and notify the designated response lead. Staff do not wash fuel into soil or drainage. A large spill, fire, strong vapor accumulation, uncontrolled leak, or exposure triggers emergency and hazardous-material response under local arrangements.

Contaminated absorbent, soil, and protective equipment are managed as directed; they are not placed in ordinary shelter waste. Exposed people receive current safety-sheet and medical direction. Incident records identify material, estimated quantity, location, source, containment, exposure, notifications, and disposition.

## Reconciliation and continuity

At each shift change, two custodians reconcile book balance, physical inventory, reserved stock, and expected demand. Variance is investigated before the next routine issue. Forecasts use equipment consumption logs rather than nameplate assumptions alone. The closeout record documents returned stock, waste, empty containers, unresolved discrepancies, and custody transfer.
