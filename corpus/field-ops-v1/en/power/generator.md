# Facilities SOP 4.2 — Portable generator deployment

**Synthetic exercise document.** This invented SOP models a municipal facility's controlled document. It is for retrieval testing, not for operating real equipment.

## Scope, roles, and go/no-go check

This procedure covers small portable generators assigned to a shelter, water point, or communications trailer. The facilities lead authorizes deployment; a named operator performs each inspection; the safety lead confirms placement. Before starting, record the generator identifier, rated output, fuel type, hour-meter reading, intended loads, weather, and operator. Do not deploy a damaged unit, a unit with a fuel leak, or equipment whose manual is unavailable to the operator.

Calculate the expected running load and starting surge. Life-safety communications, task lighting, refrigeration for essential medicine, and designated medical equipment have priority. Space heaters, cooking appliances, and convenience loads are excluded unless the site electrical plan explicitly permits them. Never exceed the lower of the generator rating, distribution equipment rating, or local operating limit.

## Placement and carbon-monoxide control

Operate portable generators outdoors at least 20 feet from occupied buildings, with exhaust directed away from doors, windows, vents, tents, queues, and neighboring structures. The generator may not be used in a garage, shed, loading bay, or partially enclosed service court, even when a door is open. Choose firm, level ground above known flood paths. Protect the unit from rain only with a manufacturer-approved canopy that preserves cooling and exhaust clearance; never improvise a tight enclosure.

Place battery carbon-monoxide alarms inside occupied areas according to the site plan. At shift change, test alarm status and document it. Headache, dizziness, nausea, confusion, or an alarm activation triggers evacuation to fresh air, emergency notification, and shutdown only if the unit can be approached safely. Ventilating the room does not authorize continued operation while the source remains active.

## Electrical connection

Connect individual appliances directly with outdoor-rated cords sized for the load, or connect through a transfer switch installed by a qualified electrician. Never feed power into building wiring through a wall outlet. Inspect plugs, insulation, grounding arrangements, cable ramps, and dry connection points. Route cords away from standing water, vehicle paths, sharp edges, and public walkways. A tripped breaker is investigated rather than repeatedly reset.

## Start, monitoring, refueling, and shutdown

Start with loads disconnected, then add approved loads in priority order while watching voltage or overload indicators. Log fuel level, operating hours, unusual noise, leaks, alarms, and load changes at least hourly. Maintain a clear exclusion zone and a suitable extinguisher. Fuel containers remain labeled and separated from ignition sources.

Before refueling, disconnect loads as the equipment procedure requires, stop the engine, and allow the unit to cool. Never add fuel to a running or hot generator. Clean small spills only under the site spill procedure; a continuing leak ends operation. For shutdown, remove loads, allow the specified cool-down run, stop the engine, close the fuel valve when applicable, and record remaining fuel and defects for the next shift.
