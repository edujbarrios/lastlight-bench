# Logistics SOP 5.7 — Public supply distribution point

**Synthetic benchmark document. Not operational guidance.** This procedure emulates a controlled distribution plan and is unsuitable for real public service.

## Mission and site design

The distribution point issues approved water, food, hygiene supplies, batteries, and other commodities while maintaining safety, equity, inventory control, and traffic flow. The site lead produces a layout before opening: public entrance, vehicle and pedestrian lanes, accessible service point, staging, counting station, loading positions, exception desk, staff refuge, first-aid contact, waste area, and emergency exit.

Separate incoming supply vehicles from the public queue. Preserve emergency access and do not block hydrants, evacuation routes, neighboring homes, or transit stops. Shade, seating, toilets, drinking water, language access, and mobility assistance are planned according to expected demand. A site without safe traffic control or an accessible service method does not open simply because stock has arrived.

## Allocation and communication

The incident logistics authority sets the issue unit, eligibility rule, opening hours, and priority exceptions. Staff apply the same current rule and do not create unofficial reserves. Public signs state what is available, the normal allocation, what information is requested, and what to do when a household cannot carry or transport the issue.

Collect only data needed for accountability. A household identifier or count may be sufficient; immigration status, diagnosis, and unrelated identity details are not recorded. The exception desk handles accessibility needs, larger households, institutional requests, damaged items, and disputes away from the main loading line. Staff never require a person to announce a private medical condition to the queue.

## Inventory controls

Receiving staff verify commodity, lot or batch where relevant, quantity, packaging condition, source, and receipt time. Staging uses first-expiring-first-out when dates apply. Keep food, water, fuel, chemicals, waste, and damaged stock separated. Each issue lane records opening balance, transfers in, units issued, damaged units, returns, and closing balance.

Physical counts occur at shift change and whenever the book balance differs from visible stock. A variance pauses discretionary issue from the affected stack while the lead checks arithmetic, transfers, damage, and possible loss. Staff do not alter earlier records to force agreement; corrections are dated and attributable.

## Queue and safety management

Greeters estimate waiting time and identify urgent accessibility or health needs without diagnosing. Controllers prevent line cutting through clear information rather than confrontation. Rising tension, heat stress, vehicle conflict, severe weather, violence, fire, or an uncontrolled spill can pause operations. A pause message explains what is known, directs people to a safe waiting area or later update, and avoids promises about reopening time.

## Closeout

Before closure, announce the final admission time and alternative information source. Reconcile stock, isolate damaged goods, remove waste, secure records, and document unmet demand. The shift report includes throughput by hour, average queue estimate, accessibility requests, exceptions, incidents, shortages, inventory variance, and recommended changes. Aggregate service counts may be published; personal records remain protected.
