# Water utility procedure 2.5 — Distribution flushing after pressure restoration

## Purpose and prerequisites

Flushing removes stagnant water and repair debris, restores disinfectant residual, and prepares a pressure zone for sampling. It does not by itself prove that water is safe and does not cancel a boil-water advisory. The field supervisor begins only after operations confirms the main is repaired, valves are in the approved configuration, the system can maintain positive pressure, and the discharge route will not create flooding, erosion, traffic hazards, or environmental damage.

The work package identifies the affected zone, source direction, valve boundaries, hydrants or blow-offs, expected flow path, discharge controls, sampling points, and critical customers. Crews compare the drawing with visible field conditions because mislabeled or inaccessible assets can invalidate the sequence. Any discrepancy is reported before operating an unverified valve.

## Planned sequence

Flush from the clean supply boundary toward the affected extremities. Open valves slowly to avoid pressure transients. Operate one planned discharge point at a time unless the hydraulic plan explicitly permits simultaneous work. Establish barriers around flowing hydrants, use diffusers where required, and keep personnel clear of hose reaction and traffic. Never direct discharge toward building entrances, electrical equipment, unstable slopes, or occupied queues.

At each location record asset identifier, start and stop time, initial and final appearance, odor observation, disinfectant residual, pressure, estimated flow, and operator. Continue until the site-specific target is met; visual clarity alone is insufficient. A sudden pressure loss, dirty-water complaint outside the mapped zone, damaged asset, uncontrolled discharge, or inability to maintain residual pauses the plan and triggers control-room review.

## Cross-connection and contamination controls

Temporary hoses and fittings are kept capped, clean, and protected from ground contact. Equipment used for sewage, firefighting foam, or non-potable service is not connected. The crew watches for submerged hydrants, unauthorized attachments, open service lines, and backflow hazards. Suspected cross-connection contamination is isolated and escalated; it is not corrected merely by extending flush duration.

Staff distinguish system flushing from household flushing. The utility may later publish premise instructions for taps, ice makers, filters, or water heaters, but field teams do not improvise those directions. Customers asking whether the water is safe are referred to the current advisory message.

## Sampling interface

Sampling begins only when the approved flush sequence is complete and operating conditions are stable. Samplers select designated taps, remove attachments when the method requires it, disinfect the outlet, observe the specified run time, and use sterile bottles without touching the cap interior. Each sample receives a unique identifier and chain-of-custody record. A sample collected before flushing completion or from the wrong point is marked invalid rather than quietly substituted.

Pressure restoration, acceptable residual, and clear appearance are operational milestones, not release criteria. The designated authority reviews laboratory results and all other evidence before changing public instructions.

## Closeout and audit

Crews return valves to the documented configuration, secure hydrants, remove barriers only after drainage is safe, and report damaged assets. The supervisor reconciles every planned point against field logs and explains omissions. The incident record links repair, flushing, sampling, complaints, and public messages so reviewers can reconstruct the exact sequence.
