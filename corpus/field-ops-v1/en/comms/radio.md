# Communications plan 3.1 — Field radio check-ins

**Synthetic exercise document.** Frequencies, channels, call signs, and timings are invented for a closed benchmark and are not an operational communications plan.

## Network purpose and discipline

The radio network supports accountability and priority logistics when mobile and internet service are unreliable. It is not a substitute for emergency dispatch. The communications lead assigns radios, call signs, battery sets, and the primary channel at briefing. Operators perform a power-on check, confirm the correct channel, inspect antenna and push-to-talk operation, and record the issue time. Plain language is preferred; personal medical details, passwords, and unnecessary names are not transmitted.

Listen before speaking. Begin with the receiving call sign, then identify the sender. Use short sentences, spell uncommon names with the agreed phonetic alphabet, and request a read-back for numbers or critical instructions. Emergency traffic has priority over scheduled reporting. Operators do not acknowledge a message they did not understand; they request a repeat or clarification.

## Hourly status format

At the top of each hour, teams transmit five fields in this order: call sign, current location, personnel status, highest-priority need, and next movement. Keep the complete transmission under 30 seconds. “Personnel status” includes team headcount and any member missing, injured, or unable to continue; sensitive clinical detail moves to an approved private route. “Next movement” includes destination and expected departure or arrival when known.

Example: “Base, this is Water Two. At North School loading dock. Four personnel, all accounted for. Need twenty empty containers. Moving to Ridge Tank at fourteen-ten. Over.” Base reads back the need and movement. The field team corrects any error immediately.

## Missed check-in and channel failure

Base logs every expected call. After a missed check-in, call the team twice over five minutes, then notify the operations lead and attempt the alternate contact method. A missed report alone is not proof of an emergency, but repeated failure combined with overdue movement activates the accountability escalation.

If the primary channel fails, wait two minutes and move to channel 6. Announce the move if the primary channel permits partial transmission. If channel 6 also fails, stop nonessential movement and return to the last physical rally point at the next even hour. “Next even hour” means, for example, 14:00 after a failure at 12:37 or 13:15. Teams do not invent a third channel because doing so fragments accountability.

## Battery and relay management

Use the lowest power that maintains reliable contact. Replace a battery at the planned threshold rather than waiting for shutdown. Label depleted batteries and keep them separate from charged stock. A designated relay may repeat messages for shadowed locations, identifying the original sender and marking the message as relayed. Relay operators do not edit the meaning.

## Logs and closeout

The base log records time, sender, location, status, request, movement, read-back, channel changes, and unresolved actions. At demobilization, teams return radios and accessories, report defects, and reconcile call signs. The communications lead reviews missed reports, congested periods, unclear messages, and battery consumption before archiving the operational log.
