# Facilities SOP 4.7 — Portable battery and charging area

**Synthetic benchmark document. Not electrical or fire-safety guidance.** Product instructions and competent authorities govern real battery systems.

## Scope and layout

This SOP covers small portable power stations, radio batteries, tool batteries, and device charging in a temporary facility. It excludes vehicle traction packs, fixed energy-storage systems, damaged electric vehicles, and industrial battery rooms. The facilities lead selects a supervised charging area away from sleeping spaces, exits, combustible storage, fuel, direct sun, water, and public traffic.

Provide noncombustible separation, ventilation, cable management, compatible power, and the detection and firefighting provisions directed by the fire authority. Do not cover chargers or place devices on bedding. Charging cables must not cross an exit or accessible route without approved protection.

## Acceptance and identification

On arrival, identify owner or asset, chemistry when known, device, rated charger, condition, state of charge if displayed, and return time. Reject or isolate equipment that is swollen, leaking, punctured, crushed, wet, unusually hot, corroded, recalled, or emitting odor, vapor, popping, or hissing. A damaged battery is not charged “to test it.”

Use the manufacturer-specified compatible charger and intact cable. Adapters, exposed conductors, daisy-chained power strips, and improvised polarity changes are prohibited. High-demand equipment is checked against circuit capacity. A tripped protective device is investigated rather than repeatedly reset.

## Charging and monitoring

Place equipment so heat can dissipate and indicators remain visible. Log connection time and planned check interval. Staff inspect for temperature rise, swelling, odor, noise, discoloration, charger fault, damaged cable, or unexpected cycling. Completed devices are disconnected when required and moved to the charged-storage zone so active bays do not become unmanaged storage.

Personal devices use a separate public process from operational batteries. Priority goes to emergency communications, accessibility equipment under its approved plan, safety lighting, and incident-management devices. Staff do not promise a full charge or accept custody of irreplaceable data without explaining the local policy.

## Abnormal event

For smoke, rapid heating, venting, flame, or violent failure, activate emergency response, move people away, and follow the fire plan. Do not pick up or carry a failing battery through an occupied area. Isolate power only when this can be done safely. The response method depends on chemistry and authority direction; this synthetic SOP intentionally does not prescribe an extinguishing agent.

After an event, preserve the scene, identify nearby devices and chargers, and quarantine related equipment. Ventilation or cleanup occurs under competent direction. Record asset, owner, times, observations, power source, protective-device operation, actions, exposures, and disposition.

## Shift reconciliation

At handoff, count active chargers, charged items, quarantined items, overdue collections, and available capacity. Reconcile every operational battery to a custodian. Remove unclaimed personal devices through the property process, not ordinary waste. At closure, disconnect equipment, inspect outlets and strips, archive logs, and report heat damage or repeat faults.
