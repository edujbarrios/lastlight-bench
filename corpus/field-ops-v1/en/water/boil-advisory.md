# Water utility procedure 2.3 — Boil-water advisory operations

**Synthetic exercise document. Not public-health guidance.** ChatGPT invented every organization, threshold context, role, and instruction in this corpus to resemble a controlled municipal manual for retrieval evaluation.

## Trigger and authority

The duty manager opens an incident record when distribution pressure falls below 20 psi, a main break permits intrusion, treatment performance is uncertain, a sample indicates contamination, or the health authority directs protective action. A pressure reading alone is verified against a second instrument when practical, but verification must not delay isolation of an obvious break. Affected pressure zones are mapped before notification so that an advisory is neither narrower nor broader than the evidence supports.

This simulated procedure distinguishes “boil-water advisory,” issued as a precaution while risk is evaluated, from “do not drink” and “do not use” orders that require different instructions. Staff must not substitute one message for another. Only the designated incident authority approves issue, expansion, and cancellation; field crews provide observations but do not independently declare an all-clear.

## Public protective message

Residents are instructed to bring clear water to a rolling boil for one minute, then allow it to cool in a clean covered container. At elevations above 6,500 feet, boil for three minutes. Cloudy water is first passed through a clean cloth, paper towel, or coffee filter; the filtered water is then boiled. The message covers water for drinking, preparing food and ice, brushing teeth, and infant formula. It also states the advisory area, issue time, available distribution points, accessibility support, and where an updated notice will appear.

Boiling does not remove fuels, solvents, metals, or every chemical contaminant. If chemical contamination is suspected, the water-quality lead escalates for a different protective order rather than extending the boiling time. Staff never promise safety based solely on appearance, taste, or odor.

## Operational response

Control-room staff preserve pressure and alarm records. Crews isolate the break, prevent back-siphonage where possible, disinfect repaired components under the utility procedure, flush designated points, and report pressure restoration by zone. Sampling staff use sterile containers, avoid taps with hoses or aerators where required, maintain chain of custody, and document sampling location, collector, time, disinfectant residual, and temperature.

Critical customers—including healthcare sites, shelters, food facilities, schools, and dialysis services—receive direct notification attempts. The public-information log records message version, language, channel, release time, and approval. Translations preserve the same duration, boundary, and cancellation criteria as the source message.

## Cancellation criteria

Pressure restoration does not itself cancel the advisory. The incident authority reviews repair completion, flushing, disinfectant residuals, bacteriological sample results, and health-authority requirements. Only after the defined evidence is satisfactory is a cancellation message released through the same major channels. The cancellation identifies the affected area and time, gives flushing or appliance-reset advice if approved, and explicitly supersedes the earlier notice.

## Records and lessons learned

Archive maps, pressure traces, work orders, sample custody forms, laboratory results, message versions, contact attempts, and the final approval. The after-action review compares time-to-detection, time-to-notification, boundary accuracy, language access, sample turnaround, and public questions. Training examples are marked as simulations so they cannot be mistaken for live advisories.
