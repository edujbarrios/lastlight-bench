# Shelter procedure 6.4 — Sanitation, cleaning, and illness clusters

**Synthetic exercise document. Not public-health guidance.** This invented shelter procedure provides realistic overlap with intake, water, and food-safety records for benchmark testing.

## Operating standard

The sanitation lead divides the site into food, sleeping, toilet, washing, waste, isolation, and clean-supply zones. A posted schedule names the responsible team, task, product, dilution, contact time, and verification method. Cleaning removes soil before disinfection. Staff never combine cleaning chemicals, decant products into drink containers, or use a product outside its label and safety information.

High-touch surfaces such as door hardware, rails, registration tables, toilet controls, and shared charging areas receive attention according to occupancy and current risk. Visible blood, vomit, feces, or other body fluid is isolated immediately and handled under the body-fluid procedure with appropriate protective equipment. Ordinary volunteers are not assigned an unfamiliar hazardous cleanup.

## Hand hygiene and toilets

Handwashing stations need flowing safe water, soap, a hygienic drying method, drainage that does not create standing water, lighting, and an accessible approach. Alcohol hand rub supplements but does not universally replace washing, particularly when hands are visibly soiled. Supplies are checked and logged throughout each shift.

Toilet capacity and service frequency are adjusted to headcount and accessibility needs. Cleaning equipment for toilets is color-coded or otherwise separated from kitchen and sleeping-area equipment. A blocked toilet, sewage odor, overflow, or loss of handwashing triggers closure of the affected area, barriers, facilities notification, and an alternate route. Staff do not reopen it merely because the floor appears dry.

## Waste and pest controls

Waste containers are lined, covered where appropriate, and removed before overflow. Sharps go only into approved puncture-resistant containers. Food waste is separated from clean supplies and sleeping areas. Exterior storage does not block exits or attract pests. Pest sightings, bites, damaged food packages, and droppings are logged by location and time; indiscriminate pesticide spraying in occupied space is prohibited.

## Illness surveillance

Intake and floor teams report aggregate symptoms through the designated health channel while protecting identity. Two or more epidemiologically linked people with vomiting, diarrhea, fever, rash, or respiratory symptoms in an unusual time window constitute a possible cluster for escalation in this simulation. This trigger begins assessment; it does not establish a diagnosis.

The health lead reviews onset times, locations, shared meals, water sources, activities, and vulnerable contacts. Symptomatic people receive current clinical/public-health direction and reasonable separation without stigma. Cleaning frequency, ventilation, food service, and water controls are reviewed. Public messages use approved facts and avoid naming individuals.

## Product preparation and verification

The product station displays the current label and safety sheet. Staff record product name, lot when relevant, preparer, dilution, time prepared, expiration or discard time, and required surface contact time. A stronger mixture is not presumed better. Test strips are used only for the products and ranges they are designed to measure.

## Shift handoff and closeout

The handoff covers closed toilets, supply levels, unresolved spills, waste pickup, pest findings, possible illness clusters, isolation spaces, and tasks due in the next two hours. Supervisors audit completion records against physical conditions. At demobilization, chemicals are inventoried, waste is removed through the approved route, reusable equipment is cleaned, and health records are transferred with access controls.
