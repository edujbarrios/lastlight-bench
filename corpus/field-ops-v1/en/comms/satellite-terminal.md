# Communications plan 3.4 — Portable satellite terminal deployment

**Synthetic exercise document.** Credentials, network names, service behavior, and deployment rules are intentionally fictional and contain no usable access information.

## Role in the communications architecture

The portable satellite terminal provides a secondary data path for incident reports and approved coordination when terrestrial service is unavailable. It is not the primary radio accountability channel and does not change the hourly radio check-in or channel-failure rally procedure. The communications lead authorizes activation, assigns an operator, and records the terminal identifier, kit contents, service profile, and custody.

Before travel, inventory terminal, mount, cables, power supply, battery, weather cover, grounding accessories where applicable, and printed quick-reference card. Credentials are obtained through the approved secure process and are never written in this document, spoken over open radio, photographed, or included in an ordinary situation report.

## Site selection

Choose a stable location with the required sky view, safe separation from vehicle traffic, public queues, fuel operations, standing water, and overhead electrical hazards. Maintain the manufacturer and site plan's radio-frequency exclusion area. The operator controls cable routes with ramps or barriers and avoids creating an egress or accessibility hazard. Wind, lightning, flooding, falling objects, and unstable roofs can make an otherwise clear site unacceptable.

The terminal is powered from the approved source after cables and connectors are inspected. A portable generator follows its own outdoor placement and carbon-monoxide controls; the terminal's need for connectivity does not justify moving a generator closer to the building. Battery operation is preferred during setup when it reduces cable and noise hazards.

## Establishing service

Orient and initialize the terminal using its current device instructions. Record power-on time, acquisition time, signal indication, software state, and any error code. Do not repeatedly power-cycle equipment without logging the symptom. Once connected, test the approved operational endpoint rather than a public entertainment service.

Apply bandwidth priorities: emergency coordination and accountability data first, essential logistics second, approved public-information products third. Personal streaming, automatic device backups, and unapproved software updates are disabled. Large files are compressed or deferred. Operators report availability in plain language over radio without transmitting network credentials.

## Security and privacy

Only managed devices join the operational network unless the communications lead approves an exception. Change default administrative credentials before field use and store secrets in the approved offline credential process. Keep the management interface unavailable to the public segment. Logs capture device association, administrative changes, outages, and high-volume transfers without unnecessarily collecting message content.

A suspected credential disclosure, unknown device, configuration change, or abnormal traffic causes isolation from the operational network and escalation. Deleting logs or restoring factory defaults before evidence is preserved is prohibited unless needed for an immediate safety reason.

## Loss of service and demobilization

If satellite service fails, record time, weather, power, indication, and error. Check physical connections and power safely, then follow the approved troubleshooting card. Teams continue the radio plan; they do not wait for internet restoration to make hourly check-ins. Critical queued reports are summarized over an approved voice route when possible.

At demobilization, terminate sessions, remove locally stored sensitive exports according to retention policy, power down, inspect and inventory the kit, coil cables without damage, record defects, and transfer custody. The communications lead archives availability, throughput constraints, outages, security events, and lessons learned.
