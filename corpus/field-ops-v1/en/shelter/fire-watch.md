# Shelter procedure 6.7 — Fire watch during alarm-system impairment

**Synthetic benchmark document. Not fire-safety guidance.** Real fire-watch requirements are set by the competent authority and site-specific code.

## Activation and authority

The shelter manager notifies the fire authority and facility owner when a required detection, alarm, sprinkler, standpipe, emergency-lighting, or notification function is impaired. Only the competent authority determines whether occupancy may continue and what compensating measures are required. Posting volunteers does not automatically make an unsafe building acceptable.

The impairment log identifies the affected system, discovery time, zones, cause if known, repair contact, authority notifications, occupancy restrictions, and expected review. Staff brief all shifts and mark out-of-service equipment so nobody assumes protection is available.

## Patrol design

The authorized fire-watch plan defines patrol areas, interval, route, communication method, staffing, and reporting point. Patrols cover sleeping rooms, corridors, kitchens, charging areas, electrical rooms, storage, exits, exterior exposures, and concealed or low-traffic spaces specified by the authority. The route varies enough to observe all assigned areas rather than becoming a predictable desk exercise.

Watch personnel carry a reliable communication device and have no competing duty that prevents continuous observation. They know how to report fire, initiate the site notification method, direct evacuation, and identify the assembly area. They do not fight a fire beyond their training or place themselves between occupants and an exit.

## Hazard controls

During impairment, restrict cooking, hot work, smoking, charging, fuel handling, decoration, storage in corridors, and other ignition or fuel sources as directed. Keep exits, fire doors, stairs, hydrants, and access roads clear. Extension cords are inspected and may not cross egress routes without approved protection. Fire doors are not wedged open.

Charging damaged batteries, unattended cooking, burning odor, smoke, unusual heat, blocked exit, missing extinguisher, or unauthorized flame is corrected or escalated immediately. A patrol entry records the finding and action; “all clear” is not entered for an area that was not physically checked.

## Fire or smoke response

On discovering smoke or fire, activate emergency services and the approved occupant-notification method without delaying to investigate the source. Begin evacuation using available safe routes, assist people who need support under the evacuation plan, close doors when safe, and report the location and known hazards. Elevators are not used unless specifically designed and authorized for the situation.

Accountability occurs at the assembly area using shelter records and floor-team reports. Missing-person information is given to responders; untrained staff do not re-enter. A false or uncertain alarm is treated as real until the authority determines otherwise.

## Restoration and records

Repairs are tested and accepted by authorized parties. The fire watch ends only when the competent authority releases it, not when an alarm panel merely appears normal. Archive patrol logs, missed rounds, findings, notifications, tests, repair records, and release time. The after-action review covers staffing, route completion, occupant communication, accessibility, and ignition-control failures.
