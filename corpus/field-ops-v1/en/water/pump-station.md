# Water utility procedure 2.7 — Pump-station degraded operation

**Synthetic benchmark document. Not engineering guidance.** It must not be used to operate pumps, electrical systems, or a drinking-water network.

## Entry and operating boundary

Only authorized personnel enter the station. Before entry, review remote alarms, utility power status, flooding, chemical alarms, ventilation, security, and communications. Standing water near electrical equipment, smoke, arcing, strong chemical odor, structural damage, confined-space hazards, or an active security threat prevents entry until the appropriate specialists control the hazard.

The operator identifies the station, pumps available, current wet-well or reservoir level, suction and discharge pressure, flow, valve configuration, disinfectant status, generator state, and downstream demand. Instrument readings are compared with physical indications and recent trends. A single implausible sensor is not treated as truth without verification.

## Degraded modes

Loss of one pump may permit reduced service if the remaining equipment stays within its approved operating envelope. The control-room lead sets the target pressure and cycling plan. Operators do not defeat interlocks, bypass protective trips, or repeatedly restart a unit that trips. Vibration, abnormal heat, seal leakage, unusual noise, motor odor, unstable pressure, or rapid cycling requires shutdown and maintenance assessment.

During utility failure, standby generation follows the generator and electrical transfer procedures. Confirm ventilation, fuel, transfer status, load priority, and carbon-monoxide controls before sustained operation. The pump load is not added until the generator is stable and authorized. Fuel forecasts use observed consumption and required reserve, not only the tank gauge.

## Pressure and water-quality interface

If distribution pressure approaches the incident threshold, notify the water-quality and incident leads early. A fall below the established threshold may trigger an advisory process, but the pump operator does not independently issue or cancel public guidance. Record the lowest pressure, duration, affected zone, cause, and restoration time.

Operational changes can disturb sediment or alter residuals. Follow the approved sampling and flushing plan after major configuration changes. Clear appearance and restored pressure do not prove microbiological safety. Chemical contamination, cross-connection, and backflow concerns require their own assessment.

## Manual operation and communications

Manual control is used only when authorized and when local observation can prevent overflow, dry running, overpressure, or equipment damage. Two-person verification applies to critical valve or control changes. Each change is announced to control, time-stamped, and read back. If communications fail, operators follow the preapproved safe-state instruction rather than improvising continued production.

## Handoff and recovery

The shift log records levels, pressures, flows, pump hours, starts, alarms, trips, fuel, generator hours, valve changes, samples, abnormal observations, notifications, and outstanding maintenance. Handoff occurs at the control panel with current system status confirmed. Return to automatic operation requires stable power, tested controls, correct valve position, cleared alarms with explained causes, and control-room approval.
