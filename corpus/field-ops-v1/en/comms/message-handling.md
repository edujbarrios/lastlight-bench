# Communications plan 3.2 — Formal message handling and precedence

**Synthetic benchmark document.** Message categories and workflows are fictional and must not be substituted for an organization's authorized communications plan.

## Purpose and channels

Formal messages preserve decisions, requests, and status when voice traffic is congested or multiple shifts must act on the same information. Operators distinguish informal coordination from a record message. A record message receives a unique number, origin, destination, precedence, subject, body, authorizing role, operator, and timestamps for receipt, transmission, acknowledgment, and closure.

Sensitive information uses the approved protected path. Open radio is used only for the minimum information needed for immediate operations. Credentials, unnecessary personal data, detailed medical histories, and safeguarding reports are not placed in a routine voice message. If the protected channel is unavailable, the originator decides whether urgency justifies a minimized alternative; the operator does not silently downgrade privacy.

## Precedence

In this exercise, Emergency precedence is limited to immediate threats to life or catastrophic system loss requiring action now. Priority covers time-sensitive action where delay could materially harm operations. Routine covers normal reports, forecasts, and requests that can wait. Precedence reflects consequence and time, not the sender's seniority or frustration.

Operators may question an apparently incorrect precedence but do not rewrite it without the originator's agreement. Emergency traffic interrupts lower categories. Within a category, process by received time unless the communications lead directs otherwise. A large attachment never outranks a short urgent message merely because it arrived first.

## Composition and verification

Use one subject and one requested action per message where practical. State who must do what, location, quantity, deadline, and reply requirement. Separate confirmed fact, estimate, and request. Avoid unexplained abbreviations. Numbers, names essential to action, coordinates, times, and hazardous-material identifiers receive character-by-character or word-by-word verification as appropriate.

The receiving operator reads back critical content and records corrections without erasing the original. If only part of a message arrives, mark it incomplete and request retransmission. Never infer the missing quantity or deadline from context.

## Routing and tracking

Route to the designated function rather than broadcasting widely. The message board shows number, precedence, destination, status, age, and owner without exposing protected content. Acknowledgment confirms receipt, not completion. The action owner later closes the message with result, time, and any follow-up.

Overdue Emergency or Priority messages are escalated according to the shift plan. Duplicate messages are linked, not discarded, because differences may matter. Conflicting instructions are held for command resolution; communications staff do not choose policy.

## Continuity and archive

If data service fails, use the approved voice or runner format and later reconcile identifiers. Radio check-ins continue under their separate protocol. At shift change, operators review all open high-precedence traffic, failed deliveries, privacy exceptions, and unresolved conflicts. The archive preserves original wording, corrections, routing, acknowledgment, completion, and access restrictions.
