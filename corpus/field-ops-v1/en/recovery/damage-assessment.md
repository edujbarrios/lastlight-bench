# Recovery procedure 9.1 — Rapid facility damage assessment

**Synthetic benchmark document. Not engineering guidance.** It supports retrieval evaluation only and cannot establish that a real structure is safe.

## Objective and limits

Rapid assessment identifies obvious hazards, sets access controls, and prioritizes specialist inspection. It is not a structural certification, insurance estimate, or permission to occupy. The assessment lead assigns teams with relevant training, a communications plan, check-in interval, and stop-work criteria. Teams work from outside inward and do not enter when collapse, fire, electrical, chemical, confined-space, violence, or unstable-ground hazards are uncontrolled.

Before departure, gather facility drawings, occupancy, known hazardous materials, utility locations, prior damage, flood or fire extent, and reports from emergency services. Information is marked confirmed, reported, or unknown. An old photograph or owner statement does not replace current observation.

## Exterior survey

Establish a safe approach and observe from multiple sides when possible. Record leaning walls, shifted foundations, roof deformation, fallen masonry, cracked columns, displaced beams, broken glazing, damaged stairs, unstable signs, trees, erosion, standing water, downed utilities, smoke, odor, and sounds. Use a standardized location reference and photographs with scale when safe.

Red indicators in this fictional system include active collapse, major structural displacement, fire involvement, energized contact, significant gas release, hazardous-material release, or official prohibition. They require exclusion and specialist response. Yellow indicators denote restricted access pending detailed review. Green means no obvious rapid-assessment indicator was observed; it does not guarantee safety.

## Interior survey boundary

Interior entry requires authorization, accountability, adequate light, safe egress, controlled utilities, atmospheric assessment where relevant, and no exterior indicator prohibiting entry. Do not use elevators. Avoid standing below damaged ceilings, near unsupported masonry, or on floors with unknown loading. Mark the route and continually reassess conditions.

Inspect accessible egress, fire separations, ceilings, walls, floors, stairs, water intrusion, electrical equipment, mechanical systems, sanitation, and stored hazards without dismantling components. Staff do not reset breakers, operate damaged gas valves, or move structural debris to improve the rating.

## Classification and communication

The assessment form records facility ID, date and time, assessors, weather, areas observed and not observed, evidence, photographs, classification, access restrictions, immediate controls, and referrals. Limitations are explicit. A colored placard without its written scope is incomplete.

Urgent hazards are reported immediately; routine forms follow the approved data path. Public statements avoid “safe” unless the competent authority uses that term. Conflicting assessments remain visible and are escalated rather than averaged.

## Reassessment and handoff

Aftershocks, wind, rain, fire, utility restoration, debris removal, or new cracking can invalidate the result. The placard states review triggers and owner. Shift handoff identifies excluded zones, people authorized to enter, outstanding specialists, utility status, and unresolved observations. Final occupancy is decided through the responsible building, fire, health, and operational authorities.
