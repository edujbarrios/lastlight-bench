# Logistics SOP 5.6 — Refrigerated food during a power outage

**Synthetic exercise document. Not food-safety advice.** This document was invented for benchmark evaluation and simulates a shelter kitchen continuity plan.

## Objective and first actions

The objective is to preserve a safe cold chain while producing an auditable discard decision. When power fails, note the exact time, verify whether the failure affects one circuit or the whole site, and keep refrigerator and freezer doors closed. Post a sign so staff do not open units casually. Assign one person to the temperature log and another to arrange alternate storage. Avoid moving food until a destination, insulated containers, ice, and transport are ready.

An unopened refrigerator keeps food cold for about 4 hours. A full freezer maintains temperature for about 48 hours; a half-full freezer for about 24 hours. These are planning references, not substitutes for measured product temperature. Grouping frozen packages helps retain cold. Dry ice is handled only by trained staff with ventilation and protective equipment; it is never stored in an airtight container.

## Monitoring and prioritization

Record the appliance identifier, outage start, door openings, displayed temperature, independent thermometer reading, and corrective action. Prioritize essential medication refrigeration under the separate pharmacy plan; do not mix medicines into food containers without authorization. High-risk perishable foods include meat, poultry, seafood, eggs, dairy, cooked leftovers, cut produce, and prepared dishes. Whole uncut produce, many hard cheeses, and shelf-stable sealed items follow their specific category rules rather than a blanket decision.

## Discard rule

Discard perishable refrigerated food that has been above 40°F (4°C) for 2 hours or more. If the elapsed time or reliable temperature history is unknown, the food is treated as unsafe. Never taste food to determine safety, and do not rely on smell or appearance. Mark discarded stock so it cannot return to service, record item class and quantity, and keep it separated from usable inventory pending waste removal.

Frozen food that still contains ice crystals or has remained at 40°F (4°C) or below may be evaluated for refreezing under current authority guidance, although quality may decline. Packages contaminated by floodwater, sewage, leaking chemicals, or damaged seals follow the contamination disposal procedure regardless of temperature.

## Restoration and handoff

When power returns, verify stable appliance operation before restocking. Do not reset the outage clock merely because power briefly cycles. The outgoing shift hands over outage start time, temperatures, door events, relocated inventory, generator status, ice supply, and pending discard decisions. Photographs may support the log but do not replace written readings. The kitchen lead signs the final disposition and opens a corrective action if alarms, thermometers, staffing, or backup power failed.

## Scenario notes

A refrigerator opened repeatedly during meal service cannot claim the four-hour unopened reference. A half-full freezer uses the 24-hour planning reference, not the 48-hour full-freezer reference. Food held above the threshold for two hours is discarded even if power later returns and the product cools again.
